 Challenge

AI for Wildfire: Detect, track, and predict wildfires from real-time data

Spain just had a record-breaking wildfire year. We're drowning in data - satellites, cameras, weather stations - but the hard part is pulling accurate, actionable signal out of it in real time. Build something that does exactly that.

Pick one of four tracks:

Early detection: train a model that accurately detects wildfires from cameras, satellites, or other feeds, so firefighters are alerted as early as possible.

Monitoring active fires: draw real-time perimeters of active fires from satellite data, determine the direction they're spreading, and simulate their movement.

Prediction: identify where and when fire risk is highest by analyzing weather data for high-risk conditions and days.

Values at risk: build an agentic system that identifies the infrastructure, people, and assets in danger when a fire breaks out, and helps make evacuation calls (which hospital, which school, etc.).

A strong submission works on real data from the resources below, produces results in (or near) real time, and clearly shows how a firefighter or emergency coordinator would actually use it.

 Challenge

Build an agent people can message.

Build an agent with Mastra and put it on a messaging platform people already use: Telegram, Discord, Slack, WhatsApp, Teams or iMessage. Our judge will message your agent from their own phone, cold, with no walkthrough. A chatbot that answers questions is the floor. Your agent has to do at least one thing a plain LLM wrapper can't: 1. Remember. It knows who it's talking to and what they said yesterday (Mastra memory). 2. Act. It calls tools that change something in the world, and asks for approval in the chat before doing anything risky (requireApproval renders Approve / Deny cards in the channel). 3. Run a process. A multi-step workflow that pauses for a human and resumes later. 4. Message first. A schedule or background task means it reaches out to the user, not just the other way round. Pick a real person with a real problem and build the agent they'd keep in their contacts after the weekend. Group chats count. Rules: - Built on Mastra (@mastra/core). Any model provider through the model router. - Reachable on at least one real messaging platform through Mastra Channels. - Does at least one of the four things above. - Public repo with a README. - Built during the event. - Submit the bot handle or invite link and the repo URL by the Sunday code freeze, and keep the bot running through judging. Tip: Telegram and Discord take about 15 minutes to stand up (bot token, one adapter, a cloudflared tunnel). WhatsApp Business Cloud needs Meta app setup. Don't start there.

€250 to the winning team.

Submit by Sunday 12:00 and keep the bot live until 17:30 Sunday for the remote judge. One project may also enter the Nebius and Cognition challenges.

 Challenge

Devin for X — Building the Autonomous Layer

Devin does software engineering autonomously because software has a feedback loop: write, run, test, fix. Your job is to find a domain with that same structure and build the layer that puts Devin to work inside it using our API. Build a system where the inputs are beyond just chat, that programmatically triggers Devin sessions, gives them a way to check their own work, and produces real artifacts without/limited human in the loop. Devin already has the hands — a shell, a browser, a machine. You're supplying the standard it has to meet. Build for consumers or enterprise — the more the output is something user can see, play, hold or use, the better it demos.

EXAMPLES

\- Games: Devin rewrites the game while you play; the change ships only if the build passes and the game still runs. - Hardware (consumer): Devin designs the PCB from a spec; DRC, ERC and SPICE decide whether it's real.

\- Physical AI (enterprise): Devin plans the shelf layout and robot route; your layer recomputes the physics and economics before dispatching.

\- Startup factory (enterprise): Devin scopes and builds the product; a browser test completing the checkout is the only definition of done. Devin proposes. Your layer authorizes — and sometimes refuses.

REQUIREMENTS: - Devin sessions are created and driven through the API, not the web UI. - Something in your code decides whether each output passes — a test suite, a simulator, a solver, a validator. Not a person, and not a model saying it looks good. - Failures go back to Devin and it tries again. Show us a run where the first attempt was wrong and the system fixed it on its own.

 Prize

A 6-month Devin Max subscription for each team member.
